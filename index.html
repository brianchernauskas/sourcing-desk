An engagement ends. The client's memory decays. The vendor's does not.

Proxima delivers a negotiation, banks the savings, and leaves. Eighteen months later the client renews that same contract from a standing start, against a counterparty with perfect recall of every concession it ever made. Sourcing Desk keeps the client's side of that asymmetry alive — and keeps Proxima inside a renewal cycle it currently walks away from.

01 — Thesis

What this is, and what it is not

The offering is not market research on a subscription, and it is not outsourced procurement execution. Both of those categories are occupied by larger, better-capitalised players. It is a narrow bet on one thing nobody else can sell: the continuity of the team that already ran your last deal.

Gartner can tell a client what the market pays. It cannot tell them what concession their own CSM folded on in month nine of the last negotiation, which of their business units quietly drove the overage, or which clause Proxima had to fight for and would fight for again. That knowledge exists for exactly as long as the engagement team remembers it, and today Proxima lets it evaporate.

The wedge

Continuity, not intelligence

Every competitor sells information. We sell the retained memory of a specific client's estate, held by people who negotiated it. That is unreplicable by a research firm and uneconomic for a competitor to rebuild.

It is also perishable. The wedge only exists while the original team is still reachable — which is an argument for building this now rather than later.

The real prize

The renewal calendar

Tracking every subscriber's contract expiry dates 12–24 months out gives Proxima the best pipeline signal that exists in sourcing: advance knowledge of exactly when a client is about to need help, across their whole estate.

Frame the offering as a pipeline engine that happens to be revenue-positive. That framing clears a much lower margin bar and survives scrutiny better than a standalone P&L line.

Conditional

The data flywheel

Each subscriber feeds back pricing, vendor behaviour, and renewal outcomes. That improves the benchmark asset, which improves both the subscription and the core consulting practice. Classic compounding — the shape boards like.

Entirely contingent on the confidentiality and conflicts questions in section 06. Do not present the flywheel without presenting the clearance alongside it.

Why this is not simply "recurring revenue would be nice"

Recurring revenue is the benefit, not the argument. Every consultancy wants it and most have failed to build it, for reasons that are well understood and covered in section 06. The argument has to be that Proxima holds an asset — engagement-derived client knowledge plus a working tooling layer — that is currently written off at the end of every project, and that the marginal cost of monetising it is low. That is a specific claim, and it is falsifiable. The evidence plan in section 05 is designed to falsify it before March, not to decorate it.

03 — Offering design

Four tiers, with a deliberate ceiling on each

The design principle is that every tier is capped. Unlimited advisory is how three clients end up consuming eighty percent of the capacity, and it is the single most common way this kind of offering dies inside a consultancy. Prices below are placeholders for the model in section 04 and carry no validation.

Tier What is in it Explicitly not in it Indicative price Cost to serve
0 — Nurture
Free		 Renewal calendar maintained for every past client; quarterly market digest Any individual response or advice — Near zero; automated off existing digest workflow
1 — Intelligence Benchmarks, vendor playbooks, price-movement alerts, full access to the planner tooling Named contact, response SLA, deal review $3,500 / mo Marginal — content is produced once and served many times
2 — Desk Tier 1 plus capped advisory hours per quarter, deal reviews, pre-negotiation preparation sessions Sitting at the negotiating table; owning the outcome $9,000 / mo Real and variable — this is the tier that must be capped and measured
3 — Embedded Named fractional sourcing lead, set days per month Nothing much — this is fractional staffing $25,000 / mo Effectively a staffed role; margin behaves like consulting, not software

The boundary against project work

Cannibalisation is a fair challenge and needs a one-sentence answer that holds up: the subscription tells a client what good looks like; the engagement goes and gets it. Sourcing Desk prepares, benchmarks and coaches. It does not sit at the table, does not run the process, and does not carry accountability for the landed outcome. Keeping that line clean is what stops a $150k engagement being traded down for a $9k-a-month retainer — and it is a line that has to be enforced in delivery, not just written on a slide.

Value attribution — build it in from day one

Consulting renews on measured savings. A subscription's value is diffuse, and "we stopped you getting hosed" does not survive a CFO's cost review at month twelve. Every benchmark issued gets logged, the client's landed price is captured against it, and the delta is attributed. Without that mechanism the offering will churn heavily at first renewal regardless of how good the advice was.

05 — Evidence plan

What has to be true by March

One completed engagement is the central weakness of this pitch. The board will ask how many post-engagement clients have been watched going cold, and the honest answer today is "one, partially." Everything below exists to change that answer. Items marked blocker would sink the offering if they come back wrong — do those first, while failing is still cheap.

Saving on this device only

Use the same phrase on every device you want in sync. It is never sent anywhere — it is hashed in the browser and the hash becomes the private address your ticks are stored at. There is no recovery: a different phrase is simply a different, empty checklist.

06 — Objections

What the room will ask

Rehearse these cold. The third column is the point: an answer without evidence behind it is an opinion, and the board hears a lot of those.

Objection Answer Evidence needed
"How is this different from Gartner or ClearEdge?" They sell market information. We sell retained knowledge of the client's own estate held by the team that negotiated it. Continuity, not intelligence. Verified competitor positioning; at least one client saying in their own words that continuity is what they would pay for
"Does this cannibalise project revenue?" Scope boundary is explicit: the subscription prepares and coaches, the engagement executes and owns the outcome. The subscription never sits at the table. Pilot data showing subscribers still bought project work — ideally that they bought more of it
"Who staffs it when everyone is billable?" Pooled desk on a rota with hard-capped hours per tier, plus a named relationship owner. Not a dedicated team until volume justifies one. Measured hours-per-client and, critically, the variance — the variance is what determines whether it is staffable
"Can we legally use client deal data this way?" blocker Not under current MSAs as written. Requires aggregation and anonymisation rights added going forward, plus a documented internal rule on what crosses between clients. Legal review complete; draft MSA clause in hand; confirmation that retrospective data is excluded
"What is Bain's position on a persistent benchmark asset?" blocker Unknown, and materially different from the per-engagement conflict wall that already exists. Being the person who raised it is good; having it raised at you is not. Written position from whoever owns conflicts, obtained before November
"Why you? You have done one engagement." The tooling is already built and in use by other consultants on live engagements. This is the commercial extension of something the firm already runs, not a first-timer's proposal. Internal adoption numbers — consultants using the planners, engagements they were used on, quotes from users
"They will cancel once their big renewal is done." Annual commitment, priced against a full-portfolio renewal calendar. Across a whole IT estate there is always something renewing — which is precisely what the spend diagnostic demonstrates. Renewal density analysis from the diagnostic: how many contract events per client per year
"How do we prove value at renewal?" Attribution logged per benchmark issued: what we said, what they landed, what the delta was. Built in from day one rather than reconstructed at month eleven. Attribution mechanism designed and running in the pilot, with at least one logged delta
"Is the margin good enough to be worth the distraction?" Judge it as a pipeline instrument, not a standalone P&L line. It clears a far lower bar on that basis, and the renewal calendar has value independent of subscription revenue. The model in section 04, populated with real hours data rather than assumptions
07 — Open questions

Unresolved, and who resolves them

Blocker

Bain conflicts on a standing data asset

The hands-off stance on negotiations covers engagements. A persistent database of what enterprises pay for specific software, sitting inside a Bain-owned entity, while Bain works extensively with software vendors and their PE owners, is a different question entirely.

Owner: conflicts / legal. Needed by: November, before any pilot captures data.

Blocker

MSA rights over engagement data

Almost certainly not permitted as current agreements are written. Needs a drafted clause for future MSAs and an explicit decision that historic data stays out of the asset.

Owner: legal. Needed by: November.

Decide

Where it sits commercially

Own P&L line, or attributed to the practice that sourced the client? This determines whose numbers it helps and therefore who will sponsor it. Get the answer before picking a sponsor, not after.

Decide

Who the buyer is

Procurement lead, CIO, or CFO. Procurement advisory budgets are thin and this often ends up funded out of a project budget instead. Different buyers want materially different pitches.

Find

A partner-level sponsor

VP boards are won before the room. The pilot work is also the vehicle for earning an advocate — every consultant interview in the evidence plan is a candidate conversation.

Decide

Name

Sourcing Desk, Renewal Intelligence, or Continuity. Not "Sourcing as a Service" — that name already belongs to outsourced procurement execution and will misdirect every conversation.

08 — Timeline

Six months

The sequencing matters more than the content. Evidence first, argument last — a case assembled from results in February is a different document from one written in September and defended for six months.

Sep – Oct

Clear the blockers, gather the base

Conflicts and MSA questions raised in writing. Consultant interviews run. Tooling adoption pushed inside the firm and counted.

Nov – Dec

Pilots running

Existing client instrumented free of charge. Two or three more from colleagues' past clients if the interviews surface willing ones. Every hour and question logged.

Jan

Real numbers

Pilot data replaces the assumptions in the model. Hours variance is the number that matters most. Attribution mechanism has produced at least one logged delta.

Feb

Sponsor and socialise

Partner-level advocate secured. Case walked through with leadership individually, so nobody hears it for the first time in the room.

Mar

Board

Present evidence of something already running, with an ask attached. Not a concept.
